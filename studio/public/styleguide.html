How Election Producer looks and speaks

What the product does today, written down: the colours and type of the Studio and of the graphics that go to air, the components built from them, and the rules of copy. Every value here is a token; the switcher above swaps the whole set, which is how another style guide would be dropped in.

Principles

  • Two worlds, one family. The Studio is a daylight page: cream paper, navy ink, one gold accent. Air is a night panel: near-black navy, white type, party colours. They share the navy, the gold and the sans; nothing else crosses over.
  • Colour means something. Purple is independent, red Republican, blue Democrat, yellow an AP call, green healthy, amber a warning or test data, red a fault. Those meanings never change page to page, and no decorative element borrows them.
  • Numbers line up. Every number that can change sits in tabular figures, so a crawl, a table or a status bar never jitters as a value changes.
  • One address per output. The ticker, each live screen and each chart are fixed addresses; the look of a page never depends on who opened it.
  • Say who and when. Every time shown carries its zone (ET by default); every publish carries a name. The interface never says "someone".
  • Locked, not hidden. A control a password cannot use stays visible, greyed, with a lock and a sentence saying which password it needs.

Copy

  • Names. The app is Election Producer; the product is Studio. The ticker at its fixed address is the Live Ticker; the two chart pages are Live Screen One and Two. Never "control room", never "on air" for the deployed state: that is production, and the verb is publish.
  • Plain English, present tense. Say what will happen and to what: "electionproducer.com/ticker takes the builder's races." No jargon on any page a producer sees; a message a producer cannot act on is a bug.
  • Every time has a zone. 7:12:08 PM ET. Relative times ("57s ago") only beside the absolute one or in a strip that refreshes.
  • Say who. "Sam Lee published at 7:12 PM", never "someone else published".
  • Outward pages describe outputs. The landing page talks about charts and the ticker, not the pipeline, the API cadence, pixel sizes or the switcher. Those belong in the README.
  • Labels are nouns, buttons are verbs. PRODUCER ACTIONS, STANDBY; Publish to Live, Pull from Live, Promote, Return to standby.
  • Capitals. Strip labels and kickers in tracked capitals; everything else sentence case, including dialog titles ("Publish to the LIVE TICKER?" is the one exception, because LIVE TICKER is a name on screen).

One difference from the product today: this guide gives independents gold (#F9C515), where the product uses purple; selecting the theme above shows the effect.

Themes

The switcher at the top rewrites the tokens on :root, and every component on this page is drawn from them. A new style guide is a new entry in the THEMES object in this file's script: the same keys, new values. The Election Night theme is transcribed from the guide on file below; the Slate theme is a demonstration of the mechanism, not a proposal. The themes live in /g/themes.js, which the ticker, every graphic and the live screens read: a producer previews one in the Studio and pushes it, and every output repaints within seconds. The Studio's own daylight page keeps its look; only its previews change.
